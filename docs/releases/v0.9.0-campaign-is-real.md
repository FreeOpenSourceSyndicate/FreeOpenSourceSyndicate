# v0.9.0 — "the campaign is real"

**Release date:** 2026-10-25 · **Branch:** `release/v0.9.0-campaign-is-real` · **Tag:** `v0.9.0`

These instructions are **specific to this release**. If you are building `main`
or another version, use [`docs/BUILD.md`](../BUILD.md) instead.

## What is new in this version (visible difference)

A playtester who has seen v0.8.0 should notice these:

1. **The game makes sound.** Combat and menus now play original sound effects —
   a shot, a hit, an explosion, victory/defeat, and menu blips — all synthesised
   in code, with no audio files. On a machine with no audio device the game
   prints one `audio: disabled (...)` line and runs silently; that is expected.
2. **Progress survives quitting.** Press **`F5`** on the **world map** to save the
   campaign and **`F9`** to load it, so a campaign lasts across sessions (it did
   not before — v0.8.0's campaign was one sitting). The CLI also takes
   **`--save <path>`** / **`--load <path>`**.
3. **Your syndicate has a name and a badge.** Starting a new campaign opens a
   **new-game screen**: type a **syndicate name** and cycle a **logo** before the
   war begins. The choice is saved with the campaign.
4. **The map is a real campaign, not a demo.** There are **twelve regions**, and
   **every region names a mission** — **eleven distinct boards** with different
   objective kinds (eliminate, reach, survive, escort, destroy, persuade,
   assassinate, retrieve) instead of the same two boards replayed. The run ends in
   a dedicated **final confrontation** (`conclave`, carried by the deepest neutral
   region) with its own **`FINAL CONFRONTATION WON`** panel.
5. **The last two objective kinds are in play: assassinate and retrieve.**
   `decapitation` is won by killing the named Overseer while the garrison still
   lives; `extraction` and `vault` are won by picking an item up and carrying it
   out to the exit tile.
6. **The time bomb.** A fused weapon arms a bomb at the impact tile that
   detonates after a short fuse — it breaches walls in the blast, a raised shield
   still absorbs it, and civilians caught in it count as collateral. It stays
   armed even if the agent who planted it dies.
7. **Drugs are three regulated bars, not a pickup.** **`[`**/**`]`** cycle the
   active bar (adrenalin / intelligence / physical) and **`;`**/**`'`** lower /
   raise it for the current selection; the bars are drawn above the squad panel
   (amber above the neutral point = a boost, blue below = a withdrawal). A high
   adrenalin bar makes an agent faster but panicky; intelligence improves threat
   recognition and target choice; physical raises health and carry capacity. The
   R&D shop sells a **research-gated ceiling** per bar, and a bought ceiling really
   caps the deployed squad's bars.
8. **Weapon names are localised.** Run with `--lang de` and the HUD roster shows
   German weapon names instead of the authored English ones.
9. **Bigger, more detailed agent sprites.** The agent sprite sheet is re-authored
   at the **Syndicate Plus** native size (**20×28**), so agents read more like the
   original (the first slice of the v1.0.0 art target, ADR-0044).
10. **The version reads `v0.9.0`** (codename **`CAMPAIGN IS REAL`**) in the
   window title, the HUD, and the title card, so you can confirm at a glance which
   build you are running.

## What you need

- A Debian-based Linux machine (Mint, Ubuntu, Debian) — the primary, tested
  platform.
- `git`, a C++17 compiler, `cmake` (>= 3.15), and `libsdl2-dev`.
- A sound card is optional: sound effects need an audio device, but the game runs
  silently without one.
- No GPU and no external assets are required: the game draws itself and embeds
  its own font, its missions, its sprite and tile sheets, and its string tables.

### The campaign screen

While the management screen is up, the tactical simulation is frozen and the
campaign clock ticks instead:

- **`M`** — toggle between the tactical board and the world map.
- **Click** a territory to select it (its name, owner, mission, tax, and status
  show in the sidebar).
- **`-`** / **`+`** — lower / raise the selected territory's tax.
- **`B`** — open the **R&D shop**: **Up/Down** move through the research tree and
  the equipment catalogue, **Left/Right** pick the squad slot, and **Enter**
  funds a research node or buys and assigns the selected gear. **`Esc`**/`B`
  closes it.
- **`D`** — deploy the selected territory's mission (starts the tactical board
  with your bought loadout).
- **`R`** — recruit an agent, taking a fixed name from the selected region's pool.
- **`F5`** / **`F9`** — save / load the campaign (the in-play save path).
- **`Esc`** — quit.

### Saving and loading

- **`F5`** saves the campaign and **`F9`** loads it **on the world map**. On the
  tactical board the keys print a one-line hint instead — a mission is transient,
  so there is no progress to save there.
- The default slot is a per-user file (`SDL_GetPrefPath`, on Linux
  `$XDG_DATA_HOME/FOSSynd/fossynd/save.json`). An explicit `--save <path>`
  overrides it; `--load <path>` resumes that file and the keys then continue it.
- In-game saves **fail soft**: a bad path prints a message and play continues, so
  a mis-keyed save never costs you progress. The CLI `--load` is stricter: naming
  a missing file is a hard error, and a corrupt or mismatched save is rejected.

### The new-game screen

On a fresh campaign the game asks you to **name your syndicate** (type it;
**Backspace** edits; printable ASCII, up to 24 characters) and **cycle a logo**
(**Left/Right**), then **Enter** begins. Leave the name empty to keep the
data-authored default. `--load` skips the screen because the save restores your
identity; `--new-game` forces it.

## 3. Install a runnable copy (recommended)

The game is a single self-contained executable, but its missions, catalogues,
locales, and **metagame world data** are also shipped as data so they can be
edited without rebuilding. `cmake --install` puts them in the standard places:

```bash
sudo cmake --install build --prefix /usr/local
# -> /usr/local/bin/FOSSynd
# -> /usr/local/share/fossynd/missions/*.json      (every shipped mission)
# -> /usr/local/share/fossynd/world/*.json         (campaign, world, research, equipment, names)
# -> /usr/local/share/fossynd/weapons.json, agents.json, vehicles.json
# -> /usr/local/share/fossynd/lang/*.json          (en + de)
# -> /usr/local/share/fossynd/fossynd.conf.example
```

Run it from anywhere:

```bash
FOSSynd
```

It finds its mission and campaign via the built-in fallback search
(`/usr/local/share`, `/usr/share`, next to the binary, repo-relative) and falls
back to the embedded copy if none is found — so it still runs if you only copy
the binary.

## 5. Controls

### Tactical board

- **Left-click** — move the squad (or select an agent under the cursor).
- **Left-drag** — box-select every agent inside the rectangle (shift-drag adds).
- **Shift + click** — append a waypoint to the selection's route (drawn on screen).
- **Keys `1`–`4`** — recall control group 1–4; **Ctrl + `1`–`4`** assign it.
  **`5`–`8`** select the 1st–4th living agent. **`A`** selects all.
- **Right-click** (or click) an enemy — order the selection to attack it.
- **`P`** — persuade the nearest civilian with the selection (recruit a follower).
- **`F`** — release the followers of the selected agents.
- **`H`** / **`S`** / **`C`** — use a **medikit** / raise a **shield** /
  run a **scanner** pulse on the selection.
- **`[` / `]`** — cycle the active **drug bar** (adrenalin / intelligence /
  physical); **`;` / `'`** lower / raise it for the selection.
- **`E`** — hijack the nearest vehicle in range, or step out if already driving.
- **`+` / `-` or the mouse wheel** — zoom the view (1x/2x/3x, nearest-neighbour).
- **`F11`** — toggle fullscreen.
- **`Space`** — pause / resume the simulation.
- **`M`** — open the campaign management screen.
- **Arrow keys** — scroll the camera. **`R`** — restart. **`Esc`** — quit.
- **`--demo`** — attract mode: the squad fights on its own.

### Campaign management screen

- **Click** a territory to select it; **`-`**/**`+`** change its tax; **`B`** opens
  the R&D shop; **`D`** deploys; **`R`** recruits an agent; **`F5`**/**`F9`**
  save/load; **`M`** returns to the tactical board; **`Esc`** quits.

> The **shield**, **medikit**, and **scanner** are bound to **`S`**, **`H`**, and
> **`C`** on the tactical board, and also reach a mission through a bought
> **loadout** (the R&D shop). Performance **drugs** are the three regulated bars
> (**`[`/`]`**, **`;`/`'`**) rather than a consumable.

## Known limitations in v0.9.0

- The legacy single-dose **drug consumable** (the old `B` key) has not been
  retired yet (#541); both models coexist for now.
- The **world map names eleven of the fifteen shipped missions**. The other
  boards are playable via `--mission` (the region wiring continues, #550).
- The **R&D shop sells a subset** of the weapon catalogue; several weapon families
  exist in `weapons.json` and are used by missions and enemies but are not yet
  purchasable in a campaign.
- **Vehicles** are modelled, drawn, and hijackable (**`E`**), and the shipped
  `precinct` mission places one, but no campaign region deploys a vehicle mission.
- Only **en** and **de** locales ship; localisation beyond English is out of scope
  for v1.0.0 (ADR-0024). **Music** is out of scope; only SFX play.
- The macOS and Windows packages are best-effort (their CI lanes are
  manual-dispatch); Linux is the hard-gated platform.
