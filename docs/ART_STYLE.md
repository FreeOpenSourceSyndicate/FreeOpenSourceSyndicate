# Art Style — the v1.0.0 target

This is the single reference for **how FOSSynd's pixel art is authored**. It
fixes the native sizes, the palette, and the drawing conventions so every art
slice agrees, and so a reviewer can tell whether a sheet matches the target.

Read `docs/LEGAL.md` and `docs/ASSETS.md` first. The short version: **all art is
original** (authored by us, mostly as text — ADR-0013) and **never traced,
converted, or re-rendered** from the original games or their marketing. The
target below is a *fidelity target* (native scale, palette discipline, chunky
pixels), not a set of files to copy.

## 1. Why "Plus" fidelity

ADR-0009 sets v1.0.0 to reproduce the original's **look and feel**, and the
original's look is chunky pixel art at a low native resolution, scaled up whole.
ADR-0044 raises that target to the **Syndicate Plus** era: a little more native
detail than the 1993 base game, while staying unmistakably pixel art. So:

- Author at the **native sizes in §2**, not at display size.
- Scale up by an **integer, nearest-neighbour** factor only (§4).
- Do **not** add smooth scaling, anti-aliasing, gradients, or vector art. Those
  are v2.x.x/fork concerns (ADR-0009) and out of the v1.0.0 fence (ADR-0024).

## 2. Native sizes

The board is an isometric grid with a **64x32** cell (a 2:1 diamond; the game's
`kTileWidth`/`kTileHeight`). Everything is authored small and magnified whole.

The table gives the **Plus target** size for each group, and the size shipped
today where the two differ (an art slice raises it):

| Asset group | Target native | Today | Source | Notes |
|-------------|---------------|-------|--------|-------|
| Ground tiles (floor, wall, destructible, rubble) | **32x16** | 16x8 | `assets/tiles/ground.txt` | One tile fills its diamond; the baker scales each to the 64x32 cell. A later art slice raises it. |
| Agent (soldier) frames | **20x28** | **20x28** | `assets/sprites/agents.txt` | Front/back stand + step. The sheet's four agent frames must all be exactly this size. |
| Props (crate, barrel) | **20x24** | 14x18 | `assets/sprites/agents.txt` | A later art slice raises them. |
| Vehicles (car, train) | **28x24** | 14x18 | `assets/sprites/agents.txt` | A later art slice raises them. |

**Uniformity rule.** Every frame baked *together into one sheet* must be the
same size — the baker rejects a ragged set. Agent frames are baked as a group of
four, so all four must be 20x28; props and vehicles are each baked on their own,
so they may keep their own size until their slice raises them.

An agent frame is drawn at **60x84** screen pixels (20x28 native x the view's
`kSpriteScale` of 3), so a standing agent is taller than one tile (a 32px-tall
diamond) — it stands on its tile and rises above it, which is how a person reads
on the board. The previous 14x18 sheet drew 42x54; the Plus size is a clear step
up in detail and presence. The agent native size is pinned in code
(`game::kAgentFrameWidth`/`kAgentFrameHeight` in `src/game/Sprite.h`) and checked
at load, so a stale sheet fails the `game_sprites` guard instead of being drawn
at the wrong scale.

## 3. Palette

The text sheet (ADR-0013) uses **four colour slots** per sheet, each a role
character; the view supplies the actual colours per team at bake time.

| Char | Slot | Purpose |
|------|------|---------|
| `B` | body | the large mass: torso, arms, legs; carries the team colour |
| `T` | trim | highlight / accent: shoulder caps, chest band, helmet stripe |
| `S` | skin | face and hands |
| `D` | dark | helmet shell, outline, belt, boots, weapon, shadowed edges |
| `.` | — | transparent |
| `A` | — | transparent, marks the **anchor** (the on-tile draw point) |

The role palettes (RGB) live in `game::paletteForRole` (`src/game/Sprite.cpp`):

| Role | body | trim | skin | dark |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| Player | `#3CAA5F` | `#C8F0D2` | `#CDAA82` | `#161E18` |
| Enemy | `#AA322A` | `#F5C878` | `#CDA57D` | `#1C1616` |
| Civilian | `#969182` | `#E1E1D7` | `#D2AF87` | `#282622` |
| Follower | `#3296AF` | `#D2F5FA` | `#CDAF87` | `#141E22` |
| Props | `#8C6E46` | `#B69664` | — | `#3C2A1A` |
| Vehicles | `#78808A` | `#BEC6CD` | — | `#30363E` |

**Why four slots.** The authored-text format is deliberately tiny (ADR-0013):
four characters, one per slot. That is enough for readable team colour, trim,
skin, and a dark that doubles as outline and shadow. A deeper palette (per-slot
shading ramps) would need a format extension — treat that as a **future art
slice**, not something to smuggle into a sheet today.

## 4. Drawing conventions

- **Silhouette first.** At this size the outline *is* the read. Every solid mass
  is bounded by `D` so it separates from the tile and from a neighbour.
- **One dark outline, no black.** `D` is a near-black tinted toward the team
  colour, not pure black; it keeps the sprite from looking cut out.
- **Trim marks the top and the team.** Put `T` where light would fall — helmet
  stripe, shoulder caps, a chest band — so the team colour is legible at a
  glance and the sprite has a light source (top-left).
- **Faces are two pixels of `S` with a `D` eye.** Below the eyes a `D` jaw guard
  keeps the head from reading as a blank blob.
- **Stand/step share one body.** The two walk frames differ **only in the legs**:
  one leg planted to the ground, the other lifted and swung back. This makes the
  walk cycle read as one body striding rather than two different poses.
- **Anchor at the bottom centre.** Agent frames carry exactly one `A`, at the
  bottom-centre column, so the sprite stands on its tile. Props/vehicles carry
  none and rest on the bottom-centre by default.
- **Nearest-neighbour, integer scale.** The view magnifies authored pixels by an
  integer factor (`kSpriteScale` x `zoom_`) so every pixel stays crisp and
  square. Never a fractional or filtered scale.

## 5. What is out of scope for v1.0.0

Per ADR-0009 and the scope fence (ADR-0024): smooth/anti-aliased scaling,
gradients, dithering tricks that read as "modern", vector/SVG art, per-sprite
lighting, and higher-fidelity remasters. A fork or a v2.x.x line may pursue
them; v1.0.0 matches the original's chunky look.

## 6. Adding or changing art

1. Author the pixels as text in the relevant `assets/*.txt` sheet (ADR-0013), at
   the §2 native size for its group.
2. Keep the sheet's frames uniform in size and keep exactly one anchor on an
   agent frame.
3. Register the asset (or the changed group) in `docs/ASSETS.md`.
4. Run `ctest` — `game_sprites` (`--headless --check-sprites`) bakes the real
   sheet and fails on a wrong size, a missing frame, or a bad pixel character.
5. Confirm the pixel-art look on a screenshot (`--headless --frames N
   --screenshot out.bmp`) rather than trusting the guard alone.
